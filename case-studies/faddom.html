From gut feel to a system: GTM analytics at Faddom

Series A · agentless IT infrastructure mapping · ~50 people · 10-month fractional engagement

Faddom's sales team was prioritizing leads by hand. A lot of work, and no way to know whether they were getting it right. Marketing couldn't tie spend to pipeline. Next quarter was a guess. What started as a scoring project became a ten-month engagement that changed how the team measures its funnel.

What the data revealed

I analyzed two years of inbound leads across every dimension the CRM had: geography, industry, job title, company size, acquisition channel, and product engagement. Two findings changed how the team works.

First: Faddom doesn't have one ICP. It has two. A "bread & butter" segment (1,000–10,000 employee companies) that closes often and fast, and a "whale" segment (10,000+) with lower close rates and year-long cycles, where a single deal can transform the year. Two segments, two different playbooks.

Second: not all leads are created equal. At all.

Most of the leads sit in tiers that barely convert. Click through the views. That flip is the whole business case.

665 leads had received two years of calls and follow-ups, and produced zero revenue. Meanwhile the top tier converted at 3.8x the average, and nobody knew it existed.

What we built

Every lead now gets a score from 0 to 100 and lands in a tier, live inside Salesforce, updating as the lead moves through the funnel. Sales works the score. Marketing refines audiences against the tiers that actually close.

"I don't dig into lead details anymore. I go straight to the score."

Omer Rabinowitz, Co-Founder & Chief Growth Officer, Faddom

From a score to an operating system

The scoring model was phase one. Over ten months the work grew into a monthly rhythm the whole team reads:

  • A monthly funnel report covering spend, lead quality, demos, pipeline, and wins, in two views: operational (what happened this month) and cohort (what each month's leads eventually produced). B2B lag stops distorting the story.
  • Demo-level pipeline estimates, deliberately conservative. Deals consistently close above them, which makes the number one the team can commit to.
  • Weekly lead-quality tracking, so drift in the funnel shows up within weeks instead of quarters.
  • A dedicated enterprise view: the 10,000+ employee "whale" segment from the dual-ICP finding, tracked from lead to demo to opportunity with milestones that respect a year-long sales cycle.

Did it work? Check the forecast.

The same foundations power a pipeline forecast: lead attributes plus demo quality, rolled into expected wins and revenue. The model predicted 56 wins and $3.47M. The year closed at 57 wins and $4.15M. Off by one win, with revenue coming in higher because the deals got bigger.

What stayed after the engagement

The engagement wrapped in the summer of 2026, and this is the part worth reading closely: the system kept running. Scoring lives in Salesforce and updates itself. The marketing team's own monthly metrics are defined in the model's terms, where "good leads" means the top two tiers and pipeline targets use the demo-level estimates. The definitions became the team's shared language, and the forecasts stay on record for a scheduled follow-up review.

"I can now show up at a conference and brag about how we measure our funnel."

Omer Rabinowitz, Co-Founder & Chief Growth Officer, Faddom
